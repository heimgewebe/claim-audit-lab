Compare the claim and source at the level of the same subject, measured property, time and scope, not merely their tone. Before choosing CONTRADICTED, identify the exact counter-fact in the excerpt: the claim and counter-fact must NOT be able to both be true. A report saying causation was not tested does not show that causation did not happen. A change in one variable does not contradict a claim about a different variable without a demonstrated link. Separate a claim that the REPORT proved X from a claim that X occurred: a report disclaiming causal proof can contradict the former but leaves the latter undecided. If no incompatible counter-fact is established, choose INSUFFICIENT. Preserve SUPPORTED when the excerpt directly establishes the claim and CONTRADICTED for genuine counter-facts.
